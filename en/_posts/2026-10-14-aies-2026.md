At the AAAI/ACM Conference on AI, Ethics, and Society (**AIES 2026**), held in Malmö from 12 to 14 October 2026, Bartol Bućan presented the poster **Auditing Alignment Controllability in LLMs via Political Axes** (B. Bućan, N. Sočec, S. Isufi, M. Granić, L. Hobor, A. Krajna, M. Kovač, M. Brčić).

Political audits of language models usually reduce each model to a single point on a political compass. In deployment, what matters more is how far, and in which directions, a model's answers can be steered through the system prompt, whether set by a platform or induced from a user's history. The authors tested seven leading models (GPT-5, Claude, Grok, Gemini, DeepSeek, Kimi and Qwen) with 12 ideological personas plus an unsteered baseline on 70 Political Compass items, 63,700 responses in total.

Context explains 88–93% of the variance on the economic and society axes, model identity less than 3%: answers are highly instruction-adjustable. Models do not shift alike: some move more, and some saturate under extreme framings. The authors conclude that political-coordinate audits need steerability audits that report dispersion, symmetry, saturation and refusal floors. Prompts, data and code are public: [arXiv:2607.23519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23519), [GitHub](https://github.com/mbrcic/llm-political-steerability), [Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21489805).
